7 website mistakes that make small businesses look unprofessional

By Brijaditya SharmaPublished 24 September 20263 min read

Visitors judge your business within seconds. These seven mistakes are common, easy to miss when you see your own site every day, and usually easy to fix.

1. A headline that doesn’t say what you do

“Welcome to our website” or “Excellence in everything” tells visitors nothing. Fix: say what you do, for whom and where, in one plain sentence.

2. Hard to contact on a phone

Tiny text links, no call or WhatsApp button, or a contact page hidden in the footer. Fix: add clear, tap-friendly buttons near the top of key pages and a simple contact page.

3. Outdated information

Old prices, last year’s offers, wrong opening hours or a copyright year from years ago make people wonder if you’re still in business. Fix: review your site every few months, and keep details consistent with your Google Business Profile.

4. Slow, heavy pages

Huge images, autoplaying videos and piles of plugins make pages crawl on mobile data. Fix: compress images, remove what you don’t need, and test your site on a real phone.

5. Generic stock photos everywhere

Smiling models in suits don’t build trust for a local bakery or a freelance consultant. Fix: use real photos of you, your team, your space and your work — even good phone photos are better than generic stock.

6. No proof you do good work

No examples, no reviews, no explanation of your experience. Fix: add real project examples, genuine reviews (with permission) and a short, honest about section. Never invent testimonials or numbers.

7. Broken forms, links and buttons

A contact form that silently fails is one of the most expensive mistakes a website can make. Fix: test every form, button and link regularly — and check the spam folder when you test.

Bonus: too much, all at once

Pop-ups, sliders, flashing banners and ten different fonts make a site feel chaotic. A calm, consistent design with clear spacing looks more trustworthy.

A 10-minute review you can do today

  1. Open your site on your phone using mobile data.
  2. Read your homepage headline as if you were a stranger.
  3. Try to contact yourself using every option.
  4. Check hours, prices, phone numbers and the copyright year.
  5. Click every menu item and button.

Quick answers

Common follow-up questions.

Still unsure? Ask me anything or message me on WhatsApp.

Do I need a full redesign to fix these?

Often not. Many of these fixes are content and settings changes. A redesign makes sense when the structure itself is holding you back.

How often should I update my website?

Check key information every few months and whenever something changes in your business.

Are templates unprofessional?

Not necessarily. A well-chosen template with real content and photos can look professional. Problems start when the template hides what makes your business different.
