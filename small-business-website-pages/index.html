What pages does a small business website need?

By Brijaditya SharmaPublished 24 September 20264 min read

You don’t need a huge website to look professional. You need the right few pages, each doing a clear job. Here’s what to include — and what can wait.

Start with the customer’s questions

Before thinking about pages, list the questions a new customer asks before choosing you: What exactly do you do? Is it right for me? Can I trust you? What does it involve? How do I get in touch? Each page on your website should answer one or more of those questions clearly.

The five essential pages

1. Home

Your home page has a few seconds to tell visitors they’re in the right place. Include:

  • a clear headline saying what you do and who you do it for;
  • a short summary of your main services;
  • proof you can be trusted — examples of your work, real reviews if you have them, or your experience;
  • a clear next step, such as “Book a call” or “Request a quote”.

2. Services

Explain each service in plain language: what it is, who it’s for, what’s included and what happens next. If you offer very different services, give each one its own section or page — this also helps you appear in more search results.

3. About

People buy from people. Share who you are, why you started, your experience and how you work. A real photo of you or your team makes a big difference. Keep it honest: no invented awards, clients or numbers.

4. Gallery or work

Show, don’t tell. Photos of your products, your space, before-and-after results or completed projects help customers picture working with you. Use your own images, optimised so they load quickly on phones.

5. Contact

Make contacting you effortless. Offer more than one option — an enquiry form, WhatsApp, phone and email — and include your location, service area and opening hours where relevant.

Useful extras

PageWhen it helps
FAQYou answer the same questions again and again.
ReviewsYou have genuine reviews you have permission to share. Never invent testimonials.
PricingYour prices are fixed and simple. If every job is different, a “request a quote” approach works better.
BookingYou take appointments — a link to a trusted booking tool is often enough.
Blog or guidesYou can write helpful articles regularly that your customers search for.
PoliciesYou collect personal data through forms or take payments. A privacy policy is usually needed; terms and refund policies help set expectations. Requirements depend on where you operate.

One page or several?

A one-page website or landing page works well when you offer one main service, run a focused campaign or need something online quickly. A multi-page website is better when you offer several services, want to appear in more search results or have lots of work to show. Many small businesses start with three to five pages and add more later.

A quick pre-launch checklist

  • Every page has a unique title and short description for search engines.
  • Your phone number, WhatsApp and email links work on mobile.
  • The contact form actually delivers messages to your inbox — test it.
  • Images are compressed and have descriptive alternative text.
  • The site looks right on a phone, tablet and desktop.
  • Your business name, address and contact details are consistent everywhere.
  • You’ve checked spelling, prices and every factual claim.

Quick answers

Common follow-up questions.

Still unsure? Ask me anything or message me on WhatsApp.

How many pages should a small business website have?

Most small businesses do well with one to five pages: Home, Services, About, Gallery or Work, and Contact. You can add more as your business grows.

Do I need a privacy policy?

If your site collects personal information — for example through a contact form — a privacy policy is generally expected and often legally required. Rules depend on where you and your customers are, so check what applies to you.

Should I put prices on my website?

If your prices are simple and fixed, publishing them can save time. If every job is different, explain what affects the price and invite people to request a quote.
