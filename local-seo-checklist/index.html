What local SEO means

Local SEO is how you help people nearby find your business when they search on Google or Google Maps — for example “hair salon near me” or “bakery in [your town]”. For many service businesses it is the most valuable kind of search, because the person is often ready to act.

1. Set up and complete your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is the listing that appears on Google Maps and in local search results. It’s free, and it’s the single most important step for most local businesses.

  • Choose the most accurate primary category, plus relevant secondary ones.
  • Add your hours, service area or address, phone number and website.
  • Write a clear, honest description of what you do.
  • Upload real photos of your work, premises, products and team.
  • Keep it updated — holiday hours, new services and posts about offers or events.

2. Keep your name, address and phone number consistent

Use exactly the same business name, address and phone number on your website, Google Business Profile, social profiles and any directories. Inconsistent details confuse customers and search engines alike.

3. Give each main service a proper page

A single page that lists everything in one line is hard to rank. A page for each main service — explaining what it is, who it’s for, where you offer it and how to book — gives Google clear signals and gives customers real answers.

If you genuinely serve several nearby areas, you can mention them naturally. Avoid creating dozens of near-identical “location pages” for places you don’t really serve; that approach adds little value and can backfire.

4. Get the on-page basics right

  • A unique page title for every page, including your main service and area where relevant.
  • One clear main heading per page.
  • Useful, specific text rather than vague slogans.
  • Descriptive alternative text for images.
  • Your contact details and location clearly visible.

5. Make it fast and mobile-friendly

Most local searches happen on phones. Compress images, avoid heavy page builders where you can, and make sure buttons to call, message or get directions are easy to tap.

6. Ask for reviews — honestly

Reviews influence both customers and local rankings. Ask happy customers to leave a review and make it easy with a direct link. Reply politely to every review, including critical ones.

Never buy reviews, write fake ones, or offer rewards in exchange for positive reviews. It breaks Google’s rules, can get reviews removed or your profile restricted, and in many countries it’s illegal.

7. Get listed in relevant directories

Reputable directories and industry associations can help people find you and reinforce your details. Focus on quality and relevance, not quantity.

8. Connect Google Search Console

Google Search Console is a free tool that shows which searches your site appears for, which pages get clicks and whether Google has found any problems. Submit your sitemap and check it monthly.

How long does it take?

Some changes, like completing your Business Profile, can help relatively quickly. Ranking improvements for competitive searches usually take months and depend on your competition. Anyone who guarantees a number-one ranking is not being honest with you.

Quick answers

Common follow-up questions.

Still unsure? Ask me anything or message me on WhatsApp.

Do I need a website for local SEO, or is a Google Business Profile enough?

A profile alone can bring calls, but a website gives Google and customers much more information and helps you appear for more searches. They work best together.

Can I rank in a city where I don’t have an address?

It is harder. If you genuinely serve that area, set it as a service area on your profile and mention it naturally on your site. Don’t invent addresses.

How many reviews do I need?

There’s no magic number. A steady flow of genuine, recent reviews matters more than a big one-time batch.
