Content repurposing: how to turn one long video into weeks of posts

By Brijaditya SharmaPublished 24 September 20264 min read

What content repurposing actually means

Content repurposing is taking something you have already made — a podcast episode, a webinar, a YouTube video, a talk — and turning it into several smaller pieces that suit other platforms. One conversation becomes short vertical clips, quote graphics, a LinkedIn post and a newsletter paragraph.

It is not copying and pasting the same file everywhere. Each platform has its own format, length and viewing habits, so each piece is reshaped for where it will live.

Why it works so well for busy business owners

  • You already did the hard part. Thinking, preparing and recording take the most effort. Repurposing makes that effort go further.
  • Different people live on different platforms. Someone who will never watch a one-hour interview might watch a 40-second clip from it on Instagram — and then go looking for the full episode.
  • Consistency becomes realistic. Posting regularly is much easier when a single recording supplies material for weeks.
  • Your best ideas get repeated. Most followers miss most posts. Hearing a strong idea more than once, in different formats, helps it stick.

Which content repurposes best

Almost any long recording can be repurposed, but some formats give you more usable moments than others:

  • Interviews and podcast conversations — full of natural questions and clear answers.
  • Webinars, workshops and talks — teaching moments, frameworks and tips.
  • Livestreams and Q&A sessions — real questions from real people.
  • Long YouTube tutorials — individual steps can stand alone as short tips.

Only repurpose content you own or have permission to use — especially when other people appear in it.

A simple workflow: from one long video to weeks of posts

1. Pick one strong source

Start with a recording where you explain something useful or tell a story clearly. Audio quality matters more than video quality: people will forgive a plain background, but not muffled sound.

2. Find the moments that stand on their own

Watch or skim the transcript and mark moments that make sense without any context: a surprising opinion, a practical tip, a short story, a common mistake, or a clear answer to a question your customers ask.

3. Cut each clip around one idea

  • Open with the most interesting line — the first few seconds decide whether people keep watching.
  • Keep one idea per clip. If you need to explain two things, make two clips.
  • End cleanly, ideally with a line that invites the next step.

4. Make it work without sound

Many people scroll with the sound off. Add accurate captions, and check names and technical terms carefully — automatic captions often get them wrong.

5. Format for each platform

Vertical video suits Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, TikTok and Facebook Reels. LinkedIn works well with square or vertical clips plus a thoughtful text post. Keep an eye on each platform’s current length limits and guidelines.

6. Write a caption that adds something

Don’t just repeat what the clip says. Add context, a question to your audience, or a pointer to the full video or your website.

7. Schedule, then learn

Spread clips out over days or weeks. After a month, look at which topics and openings held attention, and choose your next source content with that in mind.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Clips that start slowly with “So, yeah…” or long introductions.
  • Cutting mid-thought so the clip doesn’t make sense on its own.
  • Posting the same file everywhere without adjusting format or captions.
  • Judging success after one or two clips. Some clips will do far better than others, and that’s normal.
  • Forgetting the goal: every clip should give interested viewers an easy way to learn more about you.

Do it yourself, or hand it over?

If you enjoy editing and have a few hours each week, you can do this yourself with a mobile or desktop editing app. If your time is better spent running your business, it is one of the easiest marketing tasks to hand over. Quick answers

Common follow-up questions.

Still unsure? Ask me anything or message me on WhatsApp.

How many clips can I get from one video?

It depends on the length and how much useful material it contains. A focused, well-structured conversation usually has several moments worth clipping. Quality matters more than quantity.

Is repurposing the same as reposting?

No. Reposting shares the same thing again. Repurposing reshapes content into new formats suited to each platform and audience.

Do I need expensive equipment?

No. Clear audio and decent lighting matter most. A phone and an inexpensive microphone are enough to start.
