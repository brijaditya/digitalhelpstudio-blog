How much does a small business website cost? What really affects the price

Why website prices vary so much

“A website” can mean a single page built from a template in an afternoon, or a large custom site with dozens of pages, original photography, a booking system and ongoing marketing. Both are websites, but they are completely different projects. The price follows the work, the skill involved and the responsibility the designer takes on.

So instead of asking “how much is a website?”, it helps to ask “how much is this website?” — and that starts with knowing what affects the price.

The six things that really affect the price

1. How many pages and sections you need

A focused one-page site or landing page takes less time than a five-page site with separate service, about, gallery and contact pages. More pages mean more layout, more writing and more testing. Most small businesses can start with one to five pages and add more later.

2. Who writes the content

Writing is often the hidden part of a website project. If you supply finished text, the job is mostly design and build. If the designer writes or rewrites your content, that’s extra skilled work — and usually worth it, because clear words sell more than clever layouts.

3. Custom design or a template

A template gives you a ready-made layout. A custom design is created around your brand, customers and content. Custom work costs more, but it helps you stand out rather than looking like thousands of other sites.

4. Features and integrations

Contact forms, WhatsApp buttons and galleries are standard. Online shops, payment checkouts, customer logins, booking systems and multi-language setups add significant time and ongoing responsibility, so they add cost.

5. Hosting, domain and renewals

Your domain name and hosting are usually paid every year to the provider. Some designers include arranging them in the quote; others expect you to buy them yourself. Either way, ask who pays the renewals and when — this is where many surprises come from.

6. Support and maintenance after launch

Some quotes include a short support period for fixes and small changes. Ongoing maintenance, updates and new pages are usually charged separately. Knowing this in advance avoids awkward conversations later.

Cheap websites vs expensive websites

A very low price can be fine for a simple need — but check what is missing. Common gaps are no written content, no mobile testing, no basic SEO, no clear ownership of files, and no support at all. On the other hand, a high price doesn’t guarantee results either. What you want is a price that clearly matches a written scope.

Questions to ask before you accept any quote

  • How many pages are included, and what is on each one?
  • Who writes the content, and how many revision rounds are included?
  • Is the design custom or based on a template?
  • Are hosting and a domain included? Who pays the yearly renewals?
  • What happens after launch — is there any support period?
  • Do I own the final website files after full payment?
  • How are payments split, and when is each part due?

Common follow-up questions.

Is a website a one-time cost?

The design and build is usually a one-time cost, but hosting and your domain renew every year, and maintenance or new features are extra. Budget for both.

Can I start small and add pages later?

Yes. Starting with a focused one-to-five-page site and growing it is a sensible approach for most small businesses.

Why don’t you publish prices?

Because two “five-page websites” can involve very different amounts of work. A tailored quote after a free demo is fairer for both sides.
